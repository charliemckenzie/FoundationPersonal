# Investment Mix

The switching mechanism for changing where a member's super is invested. It covers three steps: setting the allocation, choosing rebalancing, and (for income accounts) setting payment preferences. What comes before and after - the intro, account selection, review, success screen - is the host's responsibility, not this feature's.

This is the piece that gets reused. The same feature appears inside a standalone investment switch journey and embedded inside the Retirement Income Account application.

---

## The four scenarios

The feature behaves differently depending on two things: the **account type** and whether the member is **new or existing**.

| | Accumulation | Income |
|---|---|---|
| **New** | Allocation only | Allocation + payment preferences |
| **Existing** | Allocation shows current % | Allocation shows current % + payment preferences |

**New vs existing** changes what the allocation step looks like:
- **New** - the member has no existing mix, so only the "Allocate:" input appears.
- **Existing** - the member already has a mix, so a "Current:" column sits alongside the new inputs so they can see exactly what they are changing from.

**Accumulation vs income** changes which steps appear:
- **Accumulation** - Allocate mix, then (if 2+ options chosen) Rebalancing.
- **Income** - Allocate mix, then (if 2+ options chosen) Rebalancing, then Payment preferences.

## Allocation warning dialog (speed bump)

When the member tries to proceed past the allocation step, the flow checks their chosen mix against four scenarios. If any match, a dialog interrupts before advancing to the next step.

The dialog always offers two paths: **Continue** (proceed with the chosen mix) and **Cancel** (go back and revise). A "Get personalised financial advice" link is shown in every case.

### Scenario 1 — 100% Cash

**Trigger:** The member puts 100% of their allocation into the Cash option only.

> **"Is 100% Cash right for you?"**
> Cash typically earns lower returns than growth options and may not keep pace with inflation over time. For most members, this can significantly reduce how much super you build before retirement.

---

### Scenario 2 — 100% in a single high-volatility option

**Trigger:** The member puts 100% into any one of the following undiversified, high-volatility options: Australian Shares, International Shares (Hedged), International Shares (Unhedged), Listed Property, High Growth, or High Growth Index.

> **"100% in [Option Name]"**
> Concentrating everything in [Option Name] means your entire balance moves with that one market. A significant downturn could substantially reduce your super with nothing else to cushion the impact.

---

### Scenario 3 — Entirely defensive mix

**Trigger:** The member spreads across two or more options, but every chosen option is a defensive one (Cash, Bonds, Conservative, or Conservative Balanced). No growth element at all.

> **"Your mix is entirely defensive"**
> Your new allocation is spread across low-growth, defensive options only. These aim to protect against loss, but typically won't deliver the growth needed to build your super meaningfully over the long term.

---

### Scenario 4 — High concentration in Unlisted Assets

**Trigger:** The member allocates 50% or more to the Unlisted Assets option.

> **"High concentration in Unlisted Assets"**
> You've put [X]% into Unlisted Assets. These are less liquid than other options — in some market conditions it may be harder to switch out quickly. Consider whether this level of exposure suits your circumstances.

---

### When no dialog appears

If the member's allocation doesn't match any of the above scenarios the flow advances immediately to the next step with no interruption.

## Rebalancing step

The rebalancing step ("Keep on track") is **conditional** — it only appears when all three conditions are met:

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Applies to balance** | The change covers the existing balance (i.e. "Apply to" is set to "All" or "Balance only"). Changing future contributions only skips rebalancing. |
| **Two or more options chosen** | The member has allocated to at least two investment options. A single-option mix cannot drift, so there is nothing to rebalance. |
| **No Lifecycle in the mix** | The mix contains zero allocation to the Lifecycle strategy. The PDS explicitly excludes Lifecycle from rebalancing — this applies even if the member chose Lifecycle alongside other options. |

### When the step is skipped

- The member changed **future contributions only** (balance is untouched).
- The member chose **only one option** (e.g. 100% Balanced).
- The member included **Lifecycle** at any percentage.

In all of these cases the flow jumps from the allocation step directly to payment preferences (income accounts) or the review step (accumulation accounts).

### "Turning off" an existing rebalancing arrangement

If the member previously had automatic rebalancing set up and then selects "No, I'll manage it myself", a notice informs them that submitting this change will turn rebalancing off on their account.

## Payment preferences step

The payment preferences step is **conditional** — it only appears when all three conditions are met:

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Income account** | The selected account is a Retirement Income Account. Accumulation accounts never see this step. |
| **Applies to payments** | The change covers the payments dial (i.e. "Apply to" is set to "All" or "Payments only"). Changing the balance-only mix on an income account skips this step. |
| **Two or more options chosen** | The member has allocated to at least two investment options. With a single option, all payments are drawn from it by definition — the step is moot. |

### When the step is skipped

- Any accumulation account.
- An income account where the change covers the **balance only**.
- An income account where the member chose **only one option**.

### Payment methods

When the step does appear, the member chooses how their pension payments are drawn:

- **Proportionally across my balance** — each payment is drawn from all options in the same proportion as the current balance at the time of payment. This is pre-selected as the default.
- **From specific options by percentage** — the member sets what share of each payment comes from each option. If one option runs out, the remainder is drawn proportionally from the others.
